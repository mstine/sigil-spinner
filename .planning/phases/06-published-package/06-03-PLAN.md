<objective>
Cross the human gate, take the last free rung of the ladder, and perform the one command in
this toolchain that cannot be taken back.

Everything before this plan cost a temp directory. This plan costs a version number,
permanently — npm's unpublish policy gives a 72-hour conditional window, a 24-hour name lock
after a full unpublish, and versions are never reusable. That asymmetry is why the phase is
shaped as a ladder and why the decision checkpoint sits where it does: after `npm publish
--dry-run` has been read and before the workflow is dispatched.

The publish lands under the `next` dist-tag, not `latest`. A bare `npm install
@falkensmage/sigil-spinner` must not resolve to `1.0.0` until plan 06-04 promotes it. That
gap is the whole point — it buys a live-infrastructure review window on an irreversible
artifact. The v1.0 lesson this repeats: both real v1.0 defects passed a fully green suite and
were caught by a human looking at output.

Output: `1.0.0` live on the registry under `@next`, attested, with the evidence recorded.
