## Why this was deferred, and why the deferral is now void

The original reason: `skill/SKILL.md` instructs sessions to invoke
`npx -y @falkensmage/sigil-spinner@latest`, and `@latest` resolves to the published
**1.1.0**, which ships seven planets. Listing ten would instruct live sessions to pass
planets the published CLI rejects with `E_UNKNOWN_PLANET`.

That reasoning was sound but the conclusion was too weak. The fix does not require
waiting for a release: **state the planet list as version-dependent.** The classical
seven work on every published version; the modern three require a version later than
1.1.0. That sentence is true today and remains true after publish, so it eliminates
the skew permanently rather than deferring it to a release-day memory item.

### What the corpus contributed to each

- **Pluto — taboo.** Absent from the draft entirely; Celestial Alchemy is explicit
  ("Pluto rules all things taboo, sex, death, money, religion") and it is the substance
  behind the Scorpio/8th-house rulership. The course's verbs — *burns down and
  rebuilds*, *rises from the ashes* — replaced the draft's weaker "excavation".
- **Uranus — barriers and social justice.** The course frame ("how your generation
  breaks the rules, how you break down barriers", "attitudes towards politics and
  social justice", "the planet of revolution, of change") is more explicitly
  rule-breaking than the draft's individualist framing.
- **Neptune — the fog is constitutive.** The draft borrowed Saturn's both-poles-at-once
  construction. The corpus shows that was wrong in an interesting way: Neptune has no
  second pole to balance against. Fog IS the planet — "nothing is ever clear when we're
  talking about Neptune", "how your generation diffuses boundaries", "very diffusive,
  very foggy quality". So the row states diffusion plainly and admits the murk, rather
  than asserting a two-pole structure it never earned. Saturn's row earned its
  both-poles treatment through a worked example; Neptune does not need one.

## The three new ambiguity axes

To be added to the Selection heuristic in `SKILL.md`, which currently covers only
subject-vs-verb. Drafted by Claude from the ratified rows, then **shown to Matt in
final form and ratified verbatim** ("axes are good, ship them", 2026-08-12). Same
provenance shape as the three domain rows: Claude drafted, Matt ratified.

- **Pluto vs. Saturn** — both end things. Saturn ends by holding a line to completion;
  Pluto burns it down and rebuilds. Saturn: the structure ends. Pluto: the self is
  remade. Take Saturn when the intention wants a thing finished and durable; take Pluto
  when it wants something to actually die.
- **Neptune vs. Moon** — both interior. The Moon feels; Neptune dissolves. Moon is
  emotion, cycles, ancestry, nurture — it moves in cycles rather than lines. Neptune is
  the boundary itself going soft. Take the Moon for grief, rest, family, dream *content*;
  take Neptune when the working is about the edge between things coming apart.
- **Uranus vs. Mars** — both disruptive. Mars applies force to move a thing that will
  not move. Uranus breaks the rule the thing sits under. Take Mars when the intention
  needs a blow struck; take Uranus when the intention needs the game changed.

## The generational objection — record it, do not act on it

Celestial Alchemy classifies all three as **generational** planets: they "have less
impact on our personality than the luminaries and personal planets do", and describe
"the way that your generation shows up in the world and interacts with society".
Uranus sits ~7 years per sign, Neptune ~14, Pluto 13-25.

A sigil is a personal working on one person's stated intention, so there is a real
tension in treating explicitly non-personal instruments as peers in a selection table
whose heuristic tells sessions to choose confidently and never ask the user.

**Matt was shown this objection directly and declined to change the design**, stating
he is already aware of Celestial Alchemy's stance and wants the option to work with
these planets when he chooses. Record the tension in `correspondences.md` as a known,
accepted trade-off with that attribution. Do NOT add a tiebreak, do NOT rank the three
below the classical seven, and do NOT reopen this.
